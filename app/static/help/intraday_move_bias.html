1. What is it?

This indicator makes a guess about where price may go next. It does not tell you to buy or sell. It shows three things on the chart:

  1. Candle colours: green means price may go up, red means it may go down, plus a few special colours.
  2. Zones: red boxes are ceilings (resistance) and green boxes are floors (support).
  3. Bubbles: circles on candles where much more trading happened than usual.
Think of it like a weather forecast. "70% chance of rain" doesn't mean it will rain. In the same way, a green candle means "up is a bit more likely", not "up for sure".

2. What each candle colour means

Each candle gets its colour once it closes. The colour is a guess for the next 30 to 60 minutes. While a candle is still forming, its colour can change.

Light green
Mild up		Price is a little more likely to go up.
Grey
Neutral		No clear direction. It could go either way.
Pink
Mild down		Price is a little more likely to go down.
Red

Yellow

Orange

Blue
Rejection at a floor		Price dipped into a floor and got pushed back up. It may bounce.

How does it decide?

It mostly asks one question: "Since the market opened today, has price been going up or down, and by how much?" Studies of many markets found that the direction of the day so far often keeps going for a while (this is called intraday momentum). It also checks whether price has broken out of the first 15 minutes' high or low (the opening range).

RSI and Bollinger Bands are used for the special colours: yellow (the bands are very narrow, so price is squeezed) and orange/blue (price poked through a band or zone and came back, with RSI very high or very low).

3. Zones: ceilings and floors

Every morning the indicator looks at yesterday and picks the 2 nearest ceilings above today's opening price and the 2 nearest floors below it.

Red box = ceiling (resistance). Price often slows down or turns back down here.
Green box = floor (support). Price often slows down or turns back up here.
Grey dashed box = broken. Two candles closed beyond it, so price went through. The next level on that side then appears.
Dotted box = estimated. Price is beyond every level of the last 5 days, so a Fibonacci extension of yesterday's range is used instead.

What the names on the boxes mean

R / SR = resistance (ceiling), S = support (floor).
pPOCThe single busiest price yesterday (the most trading happened there).
pVAH / pVALThe top / bottom of yesterday's "busy area", where about 70% of the trading happened.
pHVNAnother busy price yesterday.
pHigh / pLowYesterday's highest / lowest price.
p2…p5The same, but from 2 to 5 days ago (used when yesterday has nothing near).
Pivot P, R1, S1, R2, S2Classic levels worked out from yesterday's high, low and close.
Fib 38.2% / 50% / 61.8%Fibonacci points inside yesterday's range, where pullbacks often pause.
FibExt 161.8% …Fibonacci extension: an estimated level beyond yesterday's range.
"pVAL + Fib 50%"Two kinds of level sit at the same price, so they are joined into one zone.

4. Bubbles: very busy candles

A bubble appears only on a candle with a lot more trading than normal for that time of day, and only for the top few candles. A bigger bubble means more unusual trading. The number inside (like 3.7x) means "3.7 times more than normal".

Green bubble: lots of trading on an up candle (buyers were busy).
Red bubble: lots of trading on a down candle (sellers were busy).
Purple bubble: lots of trading but price hardly moved. Buyers and sellers fought, and a big player may be quietly soaking up orders. This is a guess from candles only; we can't see real orders.
Hover your mouse over any bubble or zone. A blue box explains, in simple words, what happened, what may come next, and when the idea is wrong. Below it are the exact numbers.

The small table at the top right shows the current guess, the zones that are still live, and how often strong calls were right on this chart (checked 10 candles later).

Lesson: a purple bubble at a ceiling or floor means a big fight is happening there. Always check the "wrong if" price in the hover box.

6. How to use it, step by step

  1. Open a 3-minute chart (it works best there, with 5m a close second). Optionally keep a 15-minute chart next to it for the bigger direction.
  2. Look at the zones first: where is the nearest ceiling above and the nearest floor below?
  3. Look at the colour of the last closed candle. Dark green or red is a stronger guess than light green or pink.
  4. Near a zone, watch for orange or blue candles and bubbles. They show a fight at that level.
  5. Hover the bubble or zone and read the "wrong if" price. That is where your idea stops making sense.
  6. On yellow, wait. A move may come, but its direction is unknown.
  7. Never use the colour alone. Use your own plan, a stop loss and a position size you are comfortable with.

7. How good is it?

We checked strong calls (dark green / red) on your own Breeze data: was price higher (for green) or lower (for red) 60 minutes later? Getting it right 50% of the time is no better than tossing a coin.

1m3m5m15m30m
NIFTY (52 days)60%61%60%65%61%
NIFTY FUT (18 days)59%60%59%57%57%
BANKNIFTY (22 days)53%54%56%56%52%
This means roughly 6 right out of 10 on NIFTY, and close to a coin toss on BANKNIFTY. It was tested on only 18 to 52 days, without brokerage or slippage. Zones were only slightly better than random levels in our tests. Treat everything here as a helping hand, not a promise. Past results don't guarantee future ones.

9. Words you may see

VolumeHow much was traded.
RVOLVolume compared with normal. 2x = twice the usual.
Volume profileA count of how much was traded at each price yesterday.
VWAPThe average price of the day, weighted by volume.
RSIA 0–100 meter of how fast price moved. Above 65 is very fast up, below 35 is very fast down.
Bollinger BandsTwo lines around price. When they squeeze close together, price is quiet.
Provisional / ConfirmedProvisional = can still change (the candle is still forming). Confirmed = fixed.

Sources: Gao, Han, Li & Zhou (2018), "Market intraday momentum", Journal of Financial Economics; Fibonacci and pivot levels from standard technical-analysis practice. Up/down volume is based on the candle colour, not real buy/sell orders.
